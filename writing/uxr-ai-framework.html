UXR × AI: Distributing intelligenceUX research and AI: Distributing intelligence

What my team learned from auditing our own use of AI and from building research tools with it: three non-negotiable phases for adopting AI in UX research, prioritizing data integrity, professional skepticism, and outcomes a team can act on.

When research is buried in a deck, it dies.
When it's integrated into agentic systems, it scales.

Stop asking what AI models can do. Start asking what job we need them to perform. The objective is not to "adopt AI." It's to adopt it wisely, as research teams move toward delivering research through human-in-the-loop agentic systems.

Where this comes from

I led a small team that started with our own process. We audited where AI was already doing research work for us: drafting screeners, surveys and test plans, summarizing transcripts, and writing summary insights. Then we built and validated research tools of our own, and evaluated AI chat personas designers had built. This framework is what that work taught us.

  • Dual-agent tree testing

    A synthetic user agent works through a navigation tree by following semantic scent, and an expert agent evaluates its path against defined logic and heuristics. We ran the same tasks across several models, including Claude, Gemini and Copilot.

    What we learned: synthetic users were more persistent than real people, runs were inconsistent with each other, and results didn't line up with human tree tests.

  • Cognitive load crawler

    A crawler that fully renders JavaScript-heavy pages, extracts their text, and scores reading difficulty across a large website with the Automated Readability Index. Scores feed a dashboard so teams can track them over time.

    What we learned: a site's language is more complex than its body copy. Labels, calls to action, page sections and navigation all needed accounting for, and sites that restrict crawlers limit what it can reach.

  • Research repository

    A taxonomy and content model that turned more than 1,000 past studies into a searchable repository ready for AI retrieval. We tested it against real survey, persona and concept-test data before trusting it.

  • Chat persona evaluation

    An evaluation of AI chat personas that designers built from compiled research insights.

    What we learned: taken out of context and elaborated on, the insights turned into rigid, exaggerated stereotypes that didn't match any behavior we'd observed in people.

01Establishing utility

Start with the job, not the model. Use AI only where it closes a real knowledge gap, judge it against vetted data, and keep hard rules separate from AI guesses.

The right job, not just the right model. Identify where AI closes a real knowledge gap without producing research slop.

Utility must come before implementation. Adding AI without a specific user problem produces "vending machine insights": plausible-sounding answers with no tactical substance. Automate where signal is unreliable, missing, or too slow to reach a person. The point is not to replace the researcher, but to free them for the edge: the unsaid nuance a model can't perceive.

High-value use cases

Prioritize sources where volume already prevents human synthesis, provided each source is labeled for structural bias first. If most of a team's research comes from one country, an unconstrained model will read that country's patterns as global truths.

Data sourceStrategic use
Support logs and transcriptsSurfacing unsaid knowledge gaps and sizing service issues at scale
App store and third-party reviewsClustering feedback by theme fast enough to catch a sentiment shift while it's forming
Internal research repositoriesSynthesizing prior research by its metadata, so studies aren't run twice
Unmoderated video sessionsPulling verbatim quotes at volume, limited to what people say, not how they say it

Ground truth and the Data Operator

To prevent justifying a system after it's built, every system is judged against a held-out set of prior research: vetted "golden" data, not raw, unchecked history.

The Data Operator owns the repository's trustworthiness. Before any AI ingestion, they audit the baseline data for bias and subjective noise. Their sign-off is what lets a system be evaluated against verified data, rather than whatever happened to be sitting in a shared drive.

Hard rules vs. AI guesswork

Every implementation splits into two strict buckets. Blurring them lets a probabilistic model quietly "correct" an unfamiliar term into a more common one it has seen before.

Hard rules: deterministic

Code-enforced, structured constraints. These are the standard rules of the research craft, easy for an expert to review and audit.

Treat as fact

AI guesswork: probabilistic

Model output based on likelihood. Useful, but subject to hallucination and drift.

Treat as a hypothesis to verify

Treating a guess as a fact is a failure of leadership.

In practice. Our audit began with the jobs AI was already doing for the team, such as drafting screeners and summarizing transcripts, rather than with what the tools could do. The cognitive load crawler scores readability with a fixed formula rather than a model's judgment, so every score can be traced and audited. The repository's taxonomy was pressure-tested against real survey, persona and concept-test data, and scored for confidence, before anything was built on top of it.

02Configuration

Match each model to the shape of its task, build in safeguards against made-up answers, and focus human review where the stakes are highest.

Match the tool to the shape of the task, and enforce the trust math instead of trusting the speed.

Speed creates a false sense of reliability: the convenience trap. Every output must be grounded in the organization's authoritative data through retrieval, and every model choice matched to what the task actually is.

Model matching by task complexity

TaskModel
Extraction and recognitionLightweight, fast models with fixed output schemas, for pulling UI elements, dates or other single facts
Reasoning and clusteringHigh-capability models with large context windows, for qualitative synthesis and spotting contradictions

Trust parameters

Small error rates compound at every step of an automated pipeline. That's why these three are necessities, not preferences:

  • Null-handling. When data is missing, the model returns nothing. It never fills the gap with something made up.
  • Edge-case logic. Defined failure states, such as a tie in clustering, stop the task and flag it for a person instead of guessing through it.
  • Quarantine standards. Answer keys and test material never enter the agent's session, so there is no path to contamination.

Risk-based human review

The researcher stays the final authority. Review effort follows strategic risk rather than being spread evenly, with the most scrutiny on the smallest, highest-stakes slice of output.

RiskReviewApplies to
HighFull reviewStrategic hypotheses, executive summaries and any ambiguous data. Every claim is checked before it ships.
MediumTargeted samplingSamples of source-traced quotes are checked against the original transcript.
LowSpot checkAdministrative triage, formatting and routine wording, checked lightly at volume.

In practice. The tree-testing tool splits the job between two agents. The synthetic user's navigation is the guesswork; the expert agent's evaluation runs on defined logic and heuristics, the hard-rule side. We ran identical, standardized tasks across several models to compare how each one navigated, and treat the results as early flags on structure, never as a substitute for testing with people.

03Validation and learning

Enforce rules in structure rather than prose, test every change against a fixed evaluation set, and count success in decisions made, not output produced.

"It works" is not a finding. Enforce structure over prose, and measure success in decisions, not tokens.

Enforce constraints in structure

Models quietly ignore instructions written as prose. When a constraint matters, it goes in the code or the harness, never in the prompt alone.

  • Output schemas. Fixed output structures, such as JSON schemas, that the model cannot drift out of.
  • Scope tagging. Reports are explicitly tagged by scope, such as a single market, so a local finding can't quietly become a global one.
  • Hierarchical structure. Clear headings and metadata, so the model understands what outranks what in the source.

Versioning and evaluation sets

Every prompt change is measured against a fixed evaluation set before it's trusted. Examples of what that looks like:

ChangeWhat the evaluation showedDecision
Retrieval-grounded extractionFabricated citations disappeared, and every claim traced back to a source."No citation, no insight" became the rule for automated reports.
A higher-reasoning model for edge casesIt was noticeably better at spotting contradictory user signals.Adopted for high-risk strategic discovery only.

Success, defined by decisions

Every project documents exactly one of each: the decision it enabled, and the limitation it didn't overcome.

  • Decision. For example: "Thematic clustering of thousands of support logs got a specific bug fix prioritized right away."
  • Limitation. For example: "This model can't read expression or gesture in video sessions. Findings are linguistic only, and need a researcher to judge emotional intent."

The feedback loop: no citation, no insight

Verify, don't assume. If the model can't point to a timestamp, a quote or a raw data point, the insight is discarded before it reaches a deck.

  1. The system produces an insight.
  2. A citation check asks whether it points to a timestamp, quote or raw data point.
  3. If it does, it goes into the report. If it doesn't, it's discarded and flagged.
  4. Each flag becomes a correction to the ground-truth set the Data Operator maintains, which shapes the next insight.

In practice. We validated the tree-testing tool against human tree tests. Synthetic users kept going where real people would give up, results changed from run to run, and they rarely matched what people did. The chat personas failed a similar check: once research insights were lifted out of context and elaborated on, they became stereotypes no one had observed. In both cases, comparing against real behavior is what showed us the limit.
