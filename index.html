Choosing the method for the question

Conjoint showed people liked each feature of a purchase flow on its own. Usability testing showed they got lost when the features were combined. I pick methods by the evidence a decision needs, like using diary studies over interviews to see how people with disabilities fit connected devices into daily routines.

Growing researchers

I managed and coached a team of six early-career rapid researchers on study design, synthesis and holding up under stakeholder questions. Four moved into senior roles.

Raising the bar on rigor

I partnered with Verizon's Insights Center of Excellence on standards for sample size, data quality and representation, and wrote our accessibility guidelines for surveys and vendors.

Moving faster with AI

I lead the design and validation of the team's AI research tools, including a searchable repository of 1,043 research studies. I also wrote our framework for using AI in research. Others include synthetic user testing and an automated readability crawler for verizon.com. I also co-led an AI tree-testing platform that compares how different AI agents navigate a site.

Accessibility beyond the product

I led the Insights Association's 2025 research initiative on disability identity, ran a workshop and certification at TMRE, and presented at the CRC. I also helped the Accessible Insights Consortium double its membership, and mentored two American Foundation for the Blind interns on a study of AI assistants and assistive technology.

Telling the story

Research only matters when it changes a decision. I write memos, readouts and executive presentations that make findings simple enough to act on.
